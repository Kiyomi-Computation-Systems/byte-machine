## 1. Summary

A dispatch state that advances back to itself on a class of bytes — a token, a
header value, the body of a string — pays a tail call per byte of a run: the
end-of-input test, the read, its clauses in byte order, and generic `+` on
`pos` and `off`. The consumer prototyped a lowering by hand (P1j) in which each
such state gets a tight loop beside it, entered after the first byte of a run,
testing a class of several runs through a table, dispatching the byte that
ends the run itself, and calling the state back at the end of a feed.

The milestone builds P1j in `compile.sls` and nothing else in `src/`: no IR,
fingerprint, runtime, interpreter or verifier change, the ABI literal stays 9,
and a parser generated by 0.8.0 still loads and runs. Decision 0059 records the
design and the measurement.

## 2. What the tree already settles

- **The cost.** Since 0049 a state is a procedure and a transition a direct
  tail call; since 0050 a class is one clause per run, in ascending byte order
  (`render-byte-clauses`). A run of a state's own class is therefore a chain
  of calls into the same procedure, each re-testing the end of the feed and
  re-scanning the clauses. An HTTP header name's lowercase letter matches the
  seventh clause of `hn-loop`, after eleven comparisons
  (`generated-http.golden.sls`).
- **Which states qualify.** A state qualifies when its class holds a byte: a
  byte a transition sends back by advance, or, with a fallback that advances
  back, a byte no transition names. An edge back is not enough; a looping
  fallback on a state whose cases name all 256 bytes is never taken (a review
  found the first form of the rule, which qualified on the edge, made
  `render-machine` raise on that machine). A scratch walk of the HTTP
  fixture's normalized IR finds 14 of its 125 dispatch states (217 states in
  all) advancing to themselves on a transition and none on a fallback: the
  token class (nine runs) in five states, the request target (one run), header
  and trailer values (three runs), and six whitespace states (two runs, 9 and
  32). Measured on their IR, jsonparse has 14, and chez-httparse's message
  parser 24 looping on a transition and 3 on a fallback, 27 in all, with 5
  more in its url parser.
- **The reserved names.** `reserved-member-3` lists every local the emitted
  body binds, and the member-3 drift guard fails for any other local without
  the `%` prefix. The brief's loop parameters (`p`, `pos0`, `off0`) would
  have had to join it, which narrows the output bindings `render-machine`
  accepts — a change a caller can see. The loop spells them with names the
  list already holds (`n`, `pos`, `off`).
- **What the census can see.** The allocation audit's twin instruments every
  `(define (%s<id> ...) ...)` inside `exec` (0058 item 1). A loop is a new
  procedure entered only from its state's arm, so before this milestone the
  audit could not tell whether any input ran one: the lane stayed green with the
  loops rendered and uncounted.

## 3. Gate 0 — decision 0059

Its numbered decisions: which states qualify and what their class is; the loop
starts after a byte in the class; the loop's four tests (none, one byte, one
run, a table shared per distinct class), its exit dispatch and its end of
feed; no new
reserved name; only the emitted text changes; the allocation audit counts
loops. The parent's work order fixed the shape as P1j before the gate, so the
gate's open question was measurement: whether byte-machine's own benchmark can
tell P1j from the two alternatives the brief rejected, and what it says
against 0048's rules on this cell.

### 3.1 How it was measured

`bench/runner/benchmark.sps`, generated lane, seven rounds of 80000
iterations, pinned with `taskset -c 7` to one performance core, nothing else
running. Four trees took five turns each: `main` (a `git worktree` under
`build/`, removed after), the branch, the branch with range tests for a class of
several runs, and the branch leaving through the state. The 20 artefacts and
the scripts that read them stayed under `build/m15/bench/` (git-ignored); every
figure in 0059 and in this file was computed by those scripts.

At 2000 iterations, the count 0048 used, every median on this cell is 7 ms or
less, so 80000 was chosen to put six workloads above the floor the dispersion
gives.

### 3.2 What it found

- **This cell's thresholds**, by 0048's method: admissible at a median of
  80 ms or more (six workloads, two of them one input), material improvement
  30%, significant regression 12%.
- **P1j against `main`, as time taken:** header names and values -16.5%,
  byte-at-a-time +2.5%, the sequence workload +1.4% (A's turns spread 1.1% and
  B's 5.6%, drifting up) and pipelined requests 0.0%. The 2000-byte target's
  -6.8% is not established: B's turns spread 12.6%, one of them at +0.0%,
  against the cell's 6.0% worst span. Rule 2 is not met, and no significant
  regression was found. The owner, told so on 2026-11-09,
  admitted it on the consumer's evidence (`gsoc-2018` -41%), and it is
  recorded as an exception, as 0053 was.
- **The alternatives:** range tests for a class of several runs take 8.9% more
  time than P1j on header names and values; leaving through the state takes
  9.4% more there and 7.0% more on the sequence workload. Byte-machine's
  workloads separate both from P1j with no overlapping turns.
- **Size:** the HTTP grammar's source +4.7%, its object +2.3%; jsonparse's
  grammar, regenerated with this emitter, +10.1% (92,635 to 102,006 bytes).
- **Downstream, with this emitter** (the coordinator's measurements, recorded
  in 0059): jsonparse regenerates with its fingerprint and ABI unchanged and
  passes its suites, and in its `events` lane takes 41% less time per byte on
  `gsoc-2018` in three turns of three; chez-httparse and uriparse regenerate
  and pass 101 of 101 and 91 of 91 tests. chez-httparse's reuse lane moves
  -1.0%, -2.6% and 0% in time on its three cases, small and at the lane's
  resolution (about 2%). The four were generated before the qualifying-rule
  fix and regenerate byte-identically after it.

## 4. Slice ① — the lowering, as built

- **`compile.sls`.** `run-loop-class`, `class-runs`, `run-loop-test-kind` and
  `render-run-loop` beside `render-state-arm`; the dispatch arm's clauses take
  a `tail` that enters the loop for a run that advances back; `render-machine`
  collects the distinct table classes in state order and renders `%c<k>`
  beside `errs`. The site comment above `run-loop-class` says what the loop
  does, what was measured, and the three shapes rejected.
- **Fixture and lanes.** `loop-machine.sls` (every shape: one byte, one run,
  several runs, a shared table, a fallback that loops, a class of every byte,
  each kind of exit, three spans each open across one loop, and a second
  machine whose looping fallback no byte reaches); `differential.loop` (the
  emitted loops read back, every two-way split behind padding, byte at a time,
  interleaved parsers, finish inside each loop, class edges in the arm and the
  loop, whole traces by value, the first callback after a loop exit pausing or
  failing, a cut at every byte telling the pause after the exit from the one
  at the end-of-feed flush, and each loop's exit on a byte its clauses do not
  name, guarded so a raise is compared); `determinism.loop` (a new golden, the
  loops' shapes as one value, walks through long runs).
- **Re-baselined:** `generated-http.golden.sls`, with its constructor line —
  the fingerprint `12865529106868080404` and the ABI 9 — unchanged, as are all
  six other goldens' (compared line for line with `main`).
- **The census (0059 item 6):** loop entries counted, three rows, a witness
  of four slopes (one per loop test), a feed-count witness for the way out at
  the end of a feed, and an op count over the bytes a non-looping arm reads.
  Measured: the field machine of 0058 renders a loop on its fallback that none
  of its rows entered; it now has a witness.
- **The limits witness** loops on two of its 256 bytes: 41,518 bytes rendered
  where `main`'s emitter gives 21,663.
- **The property generator.** At the committed seed, 3 of 41 accepted machines
  rendered a loop and no trial entered one (measured by making every loop's entry
  raise: the lane stayed green). The coverage segment's last stage, `covx9`,
  now loops on a wide fallback class. After: an `off` one too many on a loop's
  exit makes 12 machines disagree, at the end of a feed 33.
- **The two emitter-private-name guards** (member-3 drift and the compile
  lane's no-declared-name witness) admit `%r<id>` and `%c<k>`, and each fixture
  renders both.
- **Mutations:** 79 passes / 711 mutations to 81 / 752. Passes 80 and 81 are
  new; Pass 77 gains thirteen, Pass 39 one, the member-3 pass one; three are
  re-anchored and two shipped pins gained their measured `got`. Every pin of
  the six affected passes was judged against every other mutant of its pass, in
  both directions.
- **Found and not fixed:** in the property-rejections pass, eight "oracle rule
  X always fires" pins also match the matching "never fires" mutants' output,
  identically on `3c56875`: inherited, not this milestone's. Recorded beside
  those pins in `SchemeMutationSuite.cmake`, with what would settle it.
- **Docs and changelog:** `docs/generating.md` describes the loop and the
  table; `CHANGELOG.md` `[Unreleased]` says to regenerate, that the ABI stays 9
  and that a 0.8.0 parser still runs.

## 5. Slice ② — the release and the close

**v0.9.0, 2026-11-09.** Every version surface moves to 0.9.0: the release
ledger's `release-version` and its test literals, a `[0.9.0]` `CHANGELOG.md`
entry with an empty `[Unreleased]` kept above it, `Akku.manifest`,
`CMakeLists.txt`'s project `VERSION`, `SECURITY.md`'s supported-versions row,
0059's status block, and the parent plan's Status line and dated disposition.
The annotated `v0.9.0` tag is applied to the merge commit.

**The ask, dispositioned.** The brief asked that a state looping back to
itself compile to a tight loop with nothing a caller can see changing, and
nothing but the emitted text: met, by 0059 and Slice ①, with no IR,
fingerprint, runtime, interpreter or verifier change and the ABI at 9. The
brief's five candidate defects (a class one byte off, `off` wrong by the bytes
read, a loop past `end`, an exit byte sent to the wrong clause, `pk` left as
`byte`) each have a mutation the suite catches or records. Its question of
whether every qualifying state should loop is answered yes, measured on this
benchmark and the consumer's. 0048's rule 2 is not met; the owner admitted
the change, as §3 records. The consumer moves its pin and regenerates next
(the brief, "After the release").

## Explicitly out of Milestone 15

- **A new node kind** (the brief's "not asked"): the loop is a property of an
  existing state.
- **An ABI change or unsafe operations.**
- **The callback step inlined into the loop** (the brief's P3): it reaches into
  the parser's slots and needs ABI 10; it waits for its own slice.

## Open, with what would settle it

- **chez-httparse's throughput beyond its lane's resolution, and uriparse's.**
  chez-httparse's reuse lane moves -1.0%, -2.6% and 0% in time on its three
  cases (0059), small and within about 2% of what its millisecond clock over
  50-60 ms can resolve; more iterations per case would settle it.
- **The 2000-byte target.** Its turns on this branch spread 12.6%; more turns,
  or more rounds, would say whether the one-range loop gains anything there.
- **`§23.3` criterion 2:** run the benchmark through the AOT launcher.
- **`§23.3` criterion 3:** a second cell; on `tarm64osx`, repeat the dispersion
  table and derive its thresholds again (0048 item 4).
